About

An environmental catalysis lab
for hazardous gases

The Clean Air Making Laboratory is an environmental catalysis group led by Prof. Jae Hwan Yang in the Department of Environmental Engineering at Chungnam National University. We work to improve air quality by breaking down and converting hazardous gases from industrial and everyday environments through catalytic reactions.

Our flagship platform is the upcycling of waste PET plastic: we turn discarded plastic into high-performance activated carbon catalysts, addressing plastic waste and air pollution at once. Building on a decade of experience treating radioactive gases at the Korea Atomic Energy Research Institute (KAERI), we also pursue applications in the nuclear field.

Research

Plastic waste into catalysts,
hazardous gases into clean air

Several lines of hazardous-gas research branch out from a single material platform. Activated carbon made from waste PET is given catalytic functionality to break down different hazardous gases — selectively, or completely.

01 / Selective Oxidation
H₂S → S

H₂S selective oxidation

Hydrogen sulfide — a source of odor and corrosion — is selectively oxidized to elemental sulfur (S) rather than fully combusted, recovering it as a resource.

Lead · Nu Ry Sagong, M.S.
02 / Complete Oxidation
C₇H₈ → CO₂

Toluene · VOC complete oxidation

We develop catalysts that fully decompose toluene, a representative VOC, at low temperature to remove VOCs from industrial exhaust.

Lead · Ji Young Park, M.S.
03 / Nuclear Application
TEDA · I

Radioactive gas · TEDA

We study catalytic oxidation of TEDA, used to capture radioactive iodine at nuclear facilities. Carried out under KHNP's K-Cloud project.

Lead · Sung Min Park, M.S.
04 / Material Platform
PET → AC

Waste-PET activated carbon

Waste PET is converted via KOH activation into porous carbon and given adsorptive and catalytic functions — the shared material base across all our research.

Join Us

We're looking for
graduate students

Whatever your undergraduate background, you're welcome if you're drawn to solving environmental problems through catalysis. We especially welcome inquiries from students at other universities.

Research that matters

Environmental catalysis sits at the heart of the carbon-neutral, circular-economy era. The clear narrative of plastic upcycling drives both motivation and strong publication potential.

Stable research funding

The lab runs on the BK21 FOUR program and collaborative projects with national institutions such as KHNP and KAERI. Details on stipends and support are shared during an interview.

A broad career network

Our graduates have moved into environmental and materials companies including PureSphere, Blutech, and the Korea Waste Association. We leverage Daejeon's proximity to the Daedeok research complex and energy enterprises.

A place to grow

A strong record of SCI-level publications, conference presentations, and close one-on-one mentoring help you build your experimental, writing, and presentation skills.

Interested?

Send a short introduction and your research interests by email. We'll be glad to arrange a lab visit and a chat.

Teaching

Teaching

We teach the fundamentals and applications of environmental engineering at both undergraduate and graduate levels. All courses run on a flipped-classroom model.

Undergraduate

Dust Control Engineering

An undergraduate course on the behavior of particulate matter in air and dust-collection technologies.

Undergraduate

Engineering Mathematics

A foundational course building the mathematical tools needed to solve engineering problems.

Graduate

Special Topics in Hazardous Gas Treatment

A graduate course on the generation of hazardous gases and their catalytic and adsorptive treatment.

Flipped Classroom — students learn from lecture videos in advance, and class time focuses on discussion and problem-solving.
